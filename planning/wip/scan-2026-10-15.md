### 143. UK IPO: Government sets out next steps on standard essential patents (response to SEP consultation)
- **Date:** 13 Oct 2026 · **Jurisdiction:** UK · **Area:** SEP / FRAND policy
- **Summary:** The IPO published the government's next steps after the SEP consultation, which ran 15 Jul to 7 Oct 2026 and drew 93 responses as IPWatchdog reports. The government will work with the judiciary on a court anchored process for early FRAND determination in England and Wales infringement proceedings. It will also run a call for evidence on a voluntary IPO led FRAND opinion service, set up a voluntary route for filing SEP information, and develop good faith licensing guidance with industry. It drops the standalone Rate Determination Track and the IPO essentiality checking service, and it will take no further action to limit injunctions for now.
- **Why it matters:** The UK keeps its court led global rate setting model and rejects new limits on injunctions, which matters to Indian handset makers and SEP holders choosing a forum.
- **Primary:** https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-sets-out-next-steps-on-standard-essential-patents · **Secondary:** https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/10/13/uk-government-explore-court-anchored-frand-determination-proceedings-sep-infringement-cases/
- **Day:** Mon · **Priority:** High

### 144. M/s Shiv Textiles v Iconic IP Interests, LLC (Supreme Court of India, CA arising out of SLP(C) No. 30412/2025)
- **Date:** 29 Sep 2026 · **Jurisdiction:** India (Supreme Court) · **Area:** Trade mark / civil procedure (Order VII Rule 11, cause of action)
- **Summary:** Shiv Textiles, owner of JOLLY RANGER LEGWEAR, sued the owner of the JOLLY RANCHER mark at the Bhavnagar Commercial Court after three cease and desist notices. The Gujarat HC (R/SCA 1543/2025) had rejected the plaint under Order VII Rule 11. The bench of Sanjay Kumar and Sanjeev Sachdeva JJ set that aside: the respondent's own notices created the cause of action, confirmed by its later Delhi HC suit over the same mark. Commercial Trade Mark Suit No. 1/2023 restored.
- **Why it matters:** Recipients of cease and desist letters in India can bring their own suits without having them struck out at the threshold.
- **Primary:** https://lawbeat.in/pdf_upload/2026/10/02/45690202573074744order29-sep-2026-2279948.pdf (SC order; opened) · **Secondary:** https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/court-delivers-an-iconic-ruling-in-jolly-rancher-trademark-dispute/ (on the Gujarat HC ruling below)
- **Day:** Tue · **Priority:** High

### 145. Syngenta Crop Protection AG v UPL Ltd (Fed. Cir. Nos. 2025-1018, -1019, nonprecedential)
- **Date:** 14 Oct 2026 · **Jurisdiction:** US (Federal Circuit) · **Area:** Patent / PTAB (PGR2023-00017), sanctions
- **Summary:** Taranto (author), Hughes and Cunningham affirmed the PTAB PGR decision that all challenged claims of UPL's US 11,445,727 (fungicidal combinations) are unpatentable. On Syngenta's cross appeal, it upheld denial of leave to file a sanctions motion over allegedly nonexistent field trials, because UPL expressly withdrew reliance on that data (safe harbour, 37 C.F.R. § 42.11(d)(2)). Spectrum Solutions adds no good faith requirement defeating an express withdrawal.
- **Why it matters:** Involves an Indian agrochemical major and shows withdrawal of contested evidence can shield a PTAB party from sanctions.
- **Primary:** https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/25-1018.OPINION.10-14-2026_2770744.pdf · **Secondary:** https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/10/14/federal-circuit-upholds-ptab-unpatentability-ruling-against-fungicide-combination-patent/
- **Day:** Wed · **Priority:** Medium

### 147. OBI orange colour mark invalidated, German Federal Court of Justice (BGH, I ZB 58/25)
- **Date:** reported 8 Oct 2026 (decision date not confirmed) · **Jurisdiction:** Germany · **Area:** Trade mark / acquired distinctiveness of colour marks
- **Summary:** The BGH dismissed OBI's appeal, so its abstract colour mark for orange (RAL 2008) for DIY retail services is invalid, following applications by Hornbach and Globus. Acquired distinctiveness generally needs a survey showing at least 50% recognition. OBI's surveys gave 45.6% (2012) and 49.6% (2020), and a rival 2021 survey at 30% raised doubt. IPKat (13 Oct) also covers the BGH "Roter Punkt" decision (I ZB 104/25).
- **Why it matters:** Clear survey benchmark for colour marks, useful comparison for Indian colour and trade dress claims.
- **Primary not opened** (https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/Zivilsenate/I_ZS/2025/I_ZB__58-25.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4) · **Secondary:** https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2026/10/colour-me-orange-german-supreme-court.html
