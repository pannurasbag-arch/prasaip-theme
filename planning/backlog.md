### 1. Inter IKEA Systems v Algemeen Vlaams Belang (C-298/23)
- **Date:** 8 September 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** EU, CJEU (Grand Chamber)
- **Area:** Trade mark
- **Summary:** Freedom of expression under Article 11 of the Charter can in principle be "due cause" for using a sign similar to a reputed mark. Simply invoking it is not enough. National courts must weigh the user's purpose and public-interest contribution against the harm to the proprietor. The case concerned a Belgian party's "IKEA-PLAN" campaign that imitated IKEA's assembly instructions, and using a mark just to borrow its reputation for a political message does not automatically prevail.
- **Why it matters:** This is the leading EU test for parody and political or commentary uses of famous marks. It is useful for Indian brand owners enforcing in the EU and for comparing with Indian parody and disparagement case law.
- **Primary (opened):** CJEU press release, https://curia.europa.eu/site/jcms/p1_1000086739 (judgment PDF: https://courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/inter-ikea-systems-bv-v-algemeen-vlaams-belang-vzw-cjeu-judgment.pdf)
- **Secondary:** https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/9/cjeu-establishes-framework-for-balancing-trademark-rights-and-freedom-of-expression
- **Day:** Thu · **Priority:** High

### 3. EPO Enlarged Board of Appeal G 1/25 (adaptation of the description)
- **Date:** 3 September 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** EPO
- **Area:** Patent
- **Summary:** After claims are amended, the description has to be adapted only where an inconsistency with the amended claims causes non-compliance with an EPC requirement, such as Art 84 clarity. Formal concordance alone is not required.

### 6. T-Mobile International AG v Controller General of Patents (2026:DHC:6266)
- **Date:** 8 August 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** India, Delhi High Court (Tushar Rao Gedela J)
- **Area:** Patent (s.3(m); relation to s.3(k))
- **Summary:** The court set out seven guidelines for the Section 3(m) "mental act" exclusion:
  - the inquiry looks only at what the claim monopolises and is kept separate from novelty and inventive step;
  - genuine product claims fall outside 3(m);
  - nominal physical steps do not save a claim that is mental at its core;
  - computer-implemented claims should be assessed under 3(k).

  The guidelines were directed to be sent to the Patent Office.
- **Why it matters:** This is India's first structured 3(m) test and it matters for software, fintech and telecom applicants. It extends the CRI line without repeating the Recentive topic.
- **Primary:** Not opened
- **Secondary:** https://www.verdictum.in/delhi-high-court/t-mobile-international-ag-and-co-kg-v-the-controller-general-of-patents-2026dhc6266-1619466 ; https://www.livelawbiz.com/amp/patent/delhi-high-court-frames-guidelines-to-distinguish-mental-acts-from-patentable-inventions-544506

### 7. Kavitha Kuruganti v PepsiCo India Holdings (2026 INSC 811)
- **Date:** 5 August 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** India, Supreme Court (Pardiwala and Vinod Chandran JJ)
- **Area:** Plant variety protection
- **Summary:** The Court declined to interfere with the Delhi HC Division Bench ruling on PepsiCo's FL 2027 potato variety registration. It held that farmers sued for infringement can invoke Section 39(1)(iv) PPVFR Act protection in those proceedings. It found no evidence that PepsiCo's suits were vexatious and said that suing to protect statutory rights is not intimidation per se.
- **Why it matters:** Agri-biotech and FMCG clients get clarity on enforcement against farmers, and farmers get confirmation of their statutory defence. It also suits an IP-basics explainer on PPVFR.
- **Primary (opened):** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/189047489/
- **Secondary:** https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/kavitha-kuruganti-v-pepsico-india-holdings-pvt-ltd-2026-insc-811-infringement-farmers-rights-1619412 ; https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/sc-upholds-pepsicos-potato-variety-registration-says-sued-farmers-can-invoke-statutory-protection

### 8. ANI Media v OpenAI OpCo (CS(COMM) 1028/2024)
- **Date:** 24 July 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** India, Delhi High Court (Amit Bansal J)
- **Area:** Copyright / AI
- **Summary:** The court dismissed ANI's interim injunction application. It upheld Indian territorial jurisdiction even though training took place on US servers. On a prima facie view it treated the storage and training use as fair dealing, found no proven memorisation or regurgitation of ANI's works, and noted that many cited outputs appeared to come from retrieval (RAG) rather than training. Final issues were left for trial.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first major Indian ruling on AI training. It sets a jurisdictional hook for foreign AI developers and a prima facie fair-dealing position, and it contrasts with GEMA v OpenAI (item 31) and the DPIIT licensing proposal (item 29).
- **Primary (opened):** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/93327052/
- **Secondary:** https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1826248/no-injunction-against-the-machine-the-delhi-high-courts-interim-ruling-in-ani-media-v-openai-and-where-it-sits-in-the-global-ai-copyright-landscape

### 9. Bartz v Anthropic: final approval of the USD 1.5 billion class settlement

### 12. InterDigital v Shenzhen Transsion (CS(COMM) 1045/2025)
- **Date:** 1 July 2026
- **Jurisdiction:** India, Delhi High Court (Tushar Rao Gedela J)
- **Area:** SEP / FRAND
- **Summary:** Transsion was ordered to deposit pro tem security, or furnish a bank guarantee, within eight weeks. The amount is one-fifth of the cumulative value of its last counter-offer over the licence period sought. The court held that producing comparable licences is not a precondition, and that the 3G/4G/5G/HEVC portfolio cannot be artificially split by geography.
