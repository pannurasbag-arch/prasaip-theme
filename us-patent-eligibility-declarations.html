US patent eligibility declarations. When technical evidence may help.

The USPTO has reminded examiners that applicants may submit factual evidence relevant to the eligibility inquiry under existing declaration practice.

Published 26 September 2026Reviewed by Sanath V Madgula7 minute read

A declaration is not a substitute for a well drafted specification. It can be valuable when the dispute turns on a technical fact that is not adequately reflected in the examination record.

USPTO guidance issued in December 2025 discusses subject matter eligibility declarations. The guidance identifies factual evidence concerning technological improvement, the state of the art and integration of a judicial exception into a practical application.

Start with the claim and specification

The first response should identify what the claim actually requires and how those requirements operate together. An eligibility argument is stronger when the specification already explains the technical problem, implementation and measurable or observable effect.

If the application describes only a business objective and adds generic computing language, later evidence may not repair the original disclosure. New matter cannot be introduced through a declaration.

When a declaration may add value

A declaration may help when the examiner and applicant disagree about a technical proposition. Examples include whether a claimed data structure changes computer operation, whether a processing arrangement reduces a defined technical burden or whether an implementation departs from routine practice at the relevant time.

A useful declaration should
  • Identify the declarant and the basis of knowledge.
  • Address a specific factual issue in the rejection.
  • Connect the evidence to particular claim language.
  • Use test data, technical documents or contemporaneous records where appropriate.
  • Avoid broad legal conclusions that belong in attorney argument.

Evidence should support a coherent prosecution position

The declaration, amendment and written argument should tell the same technical story. If the claim is amended to emphasise a particular mechanism, the declaration should explain that mechanism rather than a broader commercial benefit.

Drafting lessons for new applications

Applications in software, artificial intelligence and data processing should describe implementation choices with care. The specification should explain inputs, transformations, technical constraints, system interactions and why the claimed arrangement changes operation of the relevant technology.

Frequently asked questions

Is a declaration mandatory for an eligibility response?

No. It is a voluntary option. Many objections can be addressed through claim analysis, amendment and legal argument.

Can a declaration add new technical disclosure?

No. It cannot cure an inadequate original disclosure by adding new matter.

Who should sign?

The appropriate declarant depends on the fact to be established and the person's knowledge, qualifications and access to supporting records.
