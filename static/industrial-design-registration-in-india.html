Industrial design registration in India

A registered design protects how a product looks: its shape, configuration, pattern, ornamentation or arrangement of lines and colours. Here is what qualifies, how the process works, what it costs and how long protection lasts.

PRASA IP helps product companies, hardware startups and manufacturers in Bengaluru and across India register designs and enforce them. A design registration is often the quickest and least expensive way to stop copies of a product's look.

Design registration in India at a glance
  • Law: the Designs Act, 2000 and the Designs Rules, 2001.
  • What it protects: the visual appearance of an article, judged by the eye. Not how it works.
  • Application: Form 1, filed online with the Design Office.
  • Official filing fee: ₹1,000 for an individual, startup or small entity, ₹4,000 for other applicants.
  • Term: 10 years, extendable once by 5 years, so 15 years in total.
  • Priority: file in India within 6 months of a first filing abroad to claim its date.
  • Classification: the Locarno International Classification.

What a design registration protects

Under the Designs Act, 2000, a design is the visual appearance of an article, judged by the eye. It must be:

  • New or original, and not published or used anywhere in the world before the filing or priority date.
  • Significantly distinguishable from known designs or combinations of known designs.
  • Applied to an article by an industrial process, whether manual, mechanical or chemical.

A design registration does not protect how a product works. Functional or mechanical features need a patent, and brand names and logos need a trade mark. Many products need more than one. Our overview of the types of intellectual property explains which right covers what.

Screen designs, GUIs and icons

Graphical user interfaces and icons were refused inconsistently for years. In March 2026 the Calcutta High Court held that there is no blanket exclusion for GUIs, and the Designs Office has since published draft guidelines on how it will examine them. Before you file

  • Keep it confidential. Showing the design publicly, at a launch, online or to buyers without a confidentiality agreement, can destroy novelty. Exhibition at a notified exhibition has a limited grace period, so check before relying on it.
  • Search earlier designs. We search the Indian design register and other sources to see whether similar designs already exist.
  • Choose the class. India follows the Locarno International Classification, and the application must name the class and the article.
  • Prepare the views. Clear drawings or photographs from each relevant angle define the scope of protection, so they matter as much as the application form.

The registration process in India

  1. Filing. We file the application with the representations, the statement of novelty and any disclaimer at the Design Office, part of the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks.
  2. Examination. An examiner checks the application against the Act and may raise objections.
  3. Response. We answer objections in writing and, if needed, at a hearing. The application must be put in order for acceptance within the period set by the Designs Rules.
  4. Registration and publication. Once accepted, the design is registered and published in the official journal, and a certificate is issued.

Registration takes effect from the date of filing, or the priority date for a convention application. If you have already filed abroad, an Indian application claiming priority must be made within 6 months of the first filing.

How long protection lasts

A registered design is protected for 10 years. It can be extended once, by 5 years, if the extension fee is paid before the first term ends, giving 15 years in total. We track renewal dates for our clients.

Enforcing a registered design

Copying a registered design, or an obvious imitation of it, on the same class of article is piracy under the Designs Act. Remedies include an injunction and damages through the courts. A registered design can also be challenged by a cancellation petition, so a careful search and accurate filing make the registration stronger. Our note on the eye test in design infringement shows how courts compare designs.

Common questions

How much does design registration cost in India?

The official fee for a design application on Form 1 is ₹1,000 for an individual, startup or small entity and ₹4,000 for other applicants. Search, drawings and professional fees are extra.

How long does a registered design last in India?

10 years, extendable once by 5 years, so 15 years in total. The extension must be filed on Form 3 before the first 10 years end and costs ₹2,000 for an individual, startup or small entity and ₹8,000 for other applicants.

Can I register a design after launching the product?

Usually not. Once a design has been published or sold, it is generally no longer new. File before launch, or check the facts with us quickly if the design has already been shown.

Can one product have both a design and a patent?

Yes. A design covers the appearance and a patent covers the technical function. Hardware products often benefit from both.

Is a design registration the same as copyright?

No. Copyright in an artistic work used as a design for industrial production can be limited, so a registered design is usually the right protection for a mass-produced product's appearance.
