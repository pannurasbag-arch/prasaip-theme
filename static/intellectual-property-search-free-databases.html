Intellectual property search: free patent, trade mark and design databases

Every major IP office lets anyone search its records free of charge. This guide lists the official tools in India, the US, Europe and the UK. It also explains what a free search cannot show you.

By Sanath V MadgulaPublished 28 November 20267 minute read

How do you search for patents, trade marks and designs? Use the free official databases. In India these are the IP India public search portals. In the US they are Patent Public Search and the USPTO trade mark search. Espacenet, TMview, DesignView and the WIPO databases cover Europe and the rest of the world. The UK IPO runs its own searches.

A free search is a good first step before you file, launch a brand or design a product. It is not a clearance opinion. The sections below explain which tool to use, how to search well and where the gaps are. The IP India links were checked on 28 November 2026.

Key points
  • Patent applications stay unpublished for about 18 months from the earliest filing in India, the US and at the EPO. No database can show them. Some are never published early, such as US applications with a nonpublication request.
  • India's trade mark public search now asks for a one time password sent to an email address or mobile number. IP India also links an AI based trade mark search.
  • The USPTO replaced its old patent databases with Patent Public Search in 2022. It retired TESS, its old trade mark search, on 30 November 2023.
  • Espacenet and WIPO PATENTSCOPE search patents from many offices at once. TMview and the WIPO Global Brand Database do the same for trade marks.
  • A word search misses logos, sound alikes and translations. Use the classification systems to widen the net.

Why search before you file

A search tells you whether someone got there first. For a trade mark, an earlier similar mark for similar goods is a key ground for refusal. In India this is section 11 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999. In the EU it is Article 8 of the EU Trade Mark Regulation. The EUIPO applies it only if the earlier owner files an opposition.

For a patent, anything published anywhere in the world before your filing date can defeat novelty or inventive step. A short search can save the cost of drafting a claim that the examiner will reject on the first report. It also shows how others describe the same technology, which helps you choose search terms and claim language.

India: IP India public search

IP India runs separate portals for each right. All are free.

  • Patents. The patent public search covers published and granted Indian applications. You can search by title, abstract, complete specification, applicant, inventor and International Patent Classification (IPC). It also accepts PCT application and publication numbers.
  • Trade marks. The trade mark public search now asks users to log in with a one time password sent to an email address or mobile number. IP India's "Search Existing Trademarks" page also links a separate AI/ML trade mark search.
  • Designs. The design search covers registered Indian designs.

For trade marks, search by word, then by phonetic variants and by class. India uses the Nice Classification, with 45 classes of goods and services. IP India's own page points users to the EUIPO's class search tool to find the right class. In our searches, the device and logo results often matter as much as the word results.

United States: USPTO search tools

The USPTO moved to two newer tools in the last few years.

  • Patents. Patent Public Search replaced PubEAST, PubWEST, PatFT and AppFT in September 2022. It searches the full text of US patents and published applications.
  • Trade marks. The trade mark search system replaced TESS, which the USPTO retired on 30 November 2023. It offers basic and advanced search.

US patents use the Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC). It is more detailed than the IPC and was developed jointly by the EPO and the USPTO. A CPC code search often finds documents that use different words for the same idea.

Europe, the UK and worldwide

  • Espacenet. The EPO's Espacenet searches patent documents from offices around the world. Machine translation helps with Chinese, Japanese and Korean documents.
  • European Patent Register. It shows the procedural status of European applications and patents, including oppositions.
  • WIPO PATENTSCOPE. PATENTSCOPE publishes PCT applications in full text on the day of publication. It also holds documents from many national and regional offices.
  • TMview and DesignView. The EUIPO's TMview searches trade marks from EU offices and many partner offices. DesignView does the same for designs. For the official EU register entry, check the EUIPO's eSearch plus.
  • WIPO Global Brand Database and Global Design Database. They cover international registrations under the Madrid and Hague systems and many national collections.
  • UK. The UK Intellectual Property Office runs its own trade mark search and the Ipsum service for patent status. EU trade marks and designs registered on 1 January 2021 became UK comparable trade marks and re-registered designs. UK patent documents can also be searched on Espacenet.

China's CNIPA and Japan's J-PlatPat also offer free searches. They are useful where a product will be made or sold in those markets. Espacenet often covers their patent documents with English abstracts.

What a free search cannot show

The biggest gap is time. Patent applications are not published until 18 months from the filing or earliest priority date. In India this is section 11A of the Patents Act, 1970. In the US it is 35 U.S.C. § 122(b), and at the EPO it is Article 93 of the European Patent Convention. A competitor's application filed last year may not appear in any database today. Once published, it can still count against a later application filed in the gap. In India this follows from section 13(1)(b).

Other gaps are practical:

  • Unregistered rights. Common law trade marks, passing off rights and, in the UK and EU, unregistered designs do not appear in office databases. A web and marketplace search is needed as well.
  • Logos and shapes. A word search will not find a similar device mark. Search by figurative codes. These are the Vienna Classification in India and the EU, and Design Search Codes at the USPTO.
  • Pending designs. Indian and US design applications are not published before registration or grant.
  • Language. Prior art in Chinese, Japanese, German or Korean is often missed by English keyword searches. Classification codes and machine translation help.
  • Status. A record may show a mark or patent that has lapsed, or omit a recent assignment. Check the register entry before relying on it.

Practical steps for a first search

  • Write down the core idea first. For a patent, list the problem, the solution and the key features in plain words.
  • Search words, then codes. Find two or three relevant documents, note their IPC or CPC codes and search those codes.
  • Search every market you plan to enter. A mark that is free in India may be taken in the EU or the US.
  • Keep a log. Record the database, the search string and the date. It helps a professional searcher pick up where you stopped.
  • Get a professional search for big decisions. Before a launch, an investment or a filing abroad, a full search and a written opinion reduce the risk.

Frequently asked questions

Is IP India public search free?

Yes. The patent, trade mark and design searches on IP India cost nothing. The trade mark public search now asks for a one time password sent to an email address or mobile number.

Can I see a patent application that was filed last month?

Usually not. Patent applications are published about 18 months after the earliest filing date in India, the US and at the EPO. In India an applicant can ask for earlier publication under section 11A(2), but most do not.

Does a free search show pending trade mark applications?

Yes. Trade mark applications are usually visible soon after filing in India, the US and the EU. Check the status before treating a mark as a threat.

Is a free trade mark search enough before launching a brand?

It is a useful first screen. It does not cover unregistered marks, similar logos or marks in other languages well. For a major launch, a full clearance search and a professional opinion reduce the risk.
