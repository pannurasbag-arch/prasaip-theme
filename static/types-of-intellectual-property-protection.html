Types of intellectual property. What each right protects in India, the US and Europe.

Patents, trade marks, copyright, designs and trade secrets protect different things. The right choice depends on the asset and the market.

Updated 29 September 202610 minute read

Intellectual property is a family of separate rights. Each one protects a different kind of creation, arises in a different way and lasts for a different time. Choosing the right one starts with knowing what each right actually covers.

In the United States the common shorthand is the four types of intellectual property. These are patents, trademarks, copyrights and trade secrets. Indian law recognises those four and adds several specialised rights, including industrial designs, geographical indications, semiconductor layout designs and plant varieties. This guide explains each type under Indian law and notes how the same right works in the United States and the European Union.

What are intellectual property rights?

Intellectual property rights, often shortened to IPR, are legal rights over creations of the mind. They cover inventions, brands, creative works, designs and valuable confidential information. Each right lets its owner stop others from using the protected subject matter without permission, within the limits the law sets.

Three features are common to almost every intellectual property right. The right is exclusive, so the owner decides who may use it. It is territorial, so a right granted in India does not by itself apply in the United States or Europe. Most rights are also limited in time. Patents, designs and copyright expire, while trade marks can be renewed indefinitely and trade secrets last while they stay confidential.

The WTO Agreement on Trade Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights, known as TRIPS, sets minimum standards that member countries must provide. India, the United States and the EU member states are all WTO members, which is why the main rights look broadly similar across these jurisdictions even though the details differ.

Patents. Protection for inventions

A patent protects a technical invention, such as a product, process, machine or composition, that is new, inventive and industrially applicable. It gives the owner the right to stop others from making, using, selling or importing the claimed invention in the country that granted it.

  • India. Governed by the Patents Act, 1970. A patent must be applied for, examined and granted. The term is 20 years from the filing date. Section 3 excludes certain subject matter, including computer programmes per se, business methods and mathematical methods.
  • United States. Utility patents last 20 years from filing. Design patents protect ornamental designs and, for applications filed on or after 13 May 2015, last 15 years from grant. Plant patents cover new asexually reproduced plant varieties.
  • Europe. A European patent is granted centrally by the European Patent Office and then takes effect country by country. Since 1 June 2023 a Unitary Patent can also be registered, covering participating EU states under one right enforced before the Unified Patent Court.

Trade marks. Protection for brands

A trade mark is a sign that tells customers who stands behind goods or services. It can be a word, logo, shape, colour combination or sound. Its job is to indicate origin, so a mark that merely describes the product is hard to protect.

  • India. Governed by the Trade Marks Act, 1999. Registration lasts ten years and can be renewed indefinitely for further ten year periods. Unregistered marks are protected through the common law action of passing off, which depends on proving goodwill.
  • United States. Rights arise from use in commerce. Federal registration with the USPTO under the Lanham Act adds nationwide notice and procedural advantages. Registrations are renewed every ten years, with a declaration of continued use due between the fifth and sixth years.
  • Europe. An EU trade mark filed with the EUIPO covers all EU member states in one registration and lasts ten years, renewable. National marks remain available in each country.

Copyright. Protection for creative and software works

Copyright protects original literary, artistic, musical and dramatic works, films, sound recordings and computer software. It protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. Copyright arises automatically when the work is created.

  • India. Governed by the Copyright Act, 1957. Registration with the Copyright Office is optional but gives useful evidence of ownership. For most literary, artistic, musical and dramatic works, the term is the life of the author plus 60 years.
  • United States. The term is generally the life of the author plus 70 years. Registration is optional for protection. For United States works, registration, or a refusal by the Copyright Office, is required before suing for infringement.
  • Europe. Terms are harmonised at the life of the author plus 70 years for most works. There is no EU wide registration system.

Industrial designs. Protection for appearance

A registered design protects the visual appearance of a product, such as its shape, pattern, ornamentation or configuration. It does not protect how the product works. Function belongs to patents.

  • India. Governed by the Designs Act, 2000. The design must be new or original and must not have been published before filing. Protection lasts ten years and can be extended once by five years.
  • United States. Product appearance is protected through design patents, described above.
  • Europe. A registered EU design lasts up to 25 years in five year terms. An unregistered EU design gives three years of protection against copying from the date the design is first made available in the EU. Since 1 May 2025 the former Community design is called the EU design.

Trade secrets. Protection for confidential know how

A trade secret is commercially valuable information that is kept confidential through reasonable measures. Recipes, source code, customer lists, manufacturing parameters and pricing models are common examples. Protection lasts as long as the secret is kept, and it is lost once the information becomes public.

  • India. There is no dedicated trade secrets statute. Protection comes from contracts such as confidentiality and employment agreements and from the equitable law of breach of confidence. The Law Commission of India recommended a specific law in its 289th Report in 2024.
  • United States. The Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016 created a federal civil action. Most states have also adopted the Uniform Trade Secrets Act.
  • Europe. The Trade Secrets Directive (EU) 2016/943 sets common rules for unlawful acquisition, use and disclosure across member states.

Rights that are specific to certain goods and technologies

Geographical indications

A geographical indication identifies goods as coming from a particular place where a quality or reputation is linked to that origin. Darjeeling tea and Mysore silk are registered Indian examples. The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 governs registration, which lasts ten years and is renewable.

Semiconductor integrated circuit layout designs

The Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act, 2000 protects original layout designs of integrated circuits. Protection lasts ten years from the filing date or the date of first commercial exploitation anywhere, whichever is earlier. The register is little used, but it matters for chip design companies with Indian operations.

Plant varieties

The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 protects new plant varieties. Protection lasts 18 years for trees and vines and 15 years for other crops. The Act also preserves specific rights for farmers.

Which right fits the asset?
  • A new technical feature or process points to a patent.
  • A product name, logo or brand identity points to a trade mark.
  • Software code, content, artwork or text points to copyright.
  • A distinctive product shape or pattern points to a registered design.
  • Valuable information that competitors cannot see points to trade secret protection.
  • Most products need several rights at once. A consumer device can involve patents, a design, a brand and copyright in its software.

Frequently asked questions

What are the four types of intellectual property?

The usual list is patents, trademarks, copyrights and trade secrets. It is a convenient summary rather than a legal definition. India and the EU also protect designs, geographical indications and other specialised rights.

What is the meaning of IPR?

IPR stands for intellectual property rights. The term covers the legal rights that protect inventions, brands, creative works, designs and confidential business information.

Which intellectual property rights must be registered in India?

Patents, registered designs, geographical indications, layout designs and plant variety rights exist only through registration. Copyright arises automatically. Trade marks can be protected without registration through passing off, but registration makes enforcement far simpler.

Can one product be protected by more than one right?

Yes. A smartphone can carry thousands of patents, registered designs for its shape, trade marks for its name and logo, and copyright in its software. The rights overlap because they protect different aspects of the same product.

Does Indian protection extend to other countries?

No. Intellectual property rights are territorial. International treaties such as the Paris Convention, the PCT, the Madrid Protocol and the Hague Agreement simplify filing abroad, but protection still arises country by country or region by region.
