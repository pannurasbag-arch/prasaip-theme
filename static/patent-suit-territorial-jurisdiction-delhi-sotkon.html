Where to sue for patent infringement in India: Sotkon and nine more suits returned by Delhi

On 19 November 2026 the Delhi High Court returned ten more IP plaints, six of them with patent claims. It applied the ABD Maestro tests and stressed two filters for patent suits: leave to sue under Section 20(b) CPC, and forum conveniens. We explain what changed and where a patent suit should now be filed.

By Praneetha K RasbagPublished 26 November 20267 minute read

Where should a patent owner sue for infringement in India? Usually where the defendant is based or where the infringing product is made or sold. On 19 November 2026 the Delhi High Court returned ten IP plaints, six with patent claims, because the real dispute lay outside Delhi.

The lead case is Sotkon SP SLU v Western Imaginary Transcon Pvt Ltd & Ors, CS(COMM) 484/2021. Justice Purushaindra Kumar Kaurav decided it with nine connected suits. The plaintiffs included Novartis, Mold-Tek, HLE Glascoat and Dr Reddy's. Every plaint was returned under Order VII Rule 10 CPC, with liberty to seek directions under Order VII Rule 10A (paras 20 and 21).

The judgment applies the principles the same judge laid down on 3 November 2026 in ABD Maestro v Great Galleon (2026:DHC:9844). We covered those principles in our note on where to sue for trade mark infringement in India. This note focuses on what the new judgment adds for patents and designs.

Key points
  • Patent and design suits have no special forum rule. Jurisdiction turns on Section 20 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (para 2).
  • Adding a copyright claim does not let a patent claim ride on Section 62(2) of the Copyright Act. Each relief must find its own forum (para 10.5).
  • Where only one of several defendants is in Delhi, the plaintiff needs leave of the court or the others' acquiescence under Section 20(b). The court refused leave in Sotkon (paras 10.6 and 10.10).
  • Four patent suits were returned on forum conveniens alone. The court looked for the "material, integral and essential part of the cause of action" (para 7.11).
  • A sale by an online pharmacy to a buyer set up by the plaintiff did not create jurisdiction in the DAPLO suit (para 14.6).

Why patent suits are tested under Section 20 CPC

Section 134(2) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 lets a plaintiff sue where it carries on business. Section 62(2) of the Copyright Act, 1957 does the same. The Patents Act, 1970 and the Designs Act, 2000 have no such provision. A patent suit must therefore satisfy Section 20 CPC. Every defendant must reside or carry on business there, or any one may with leave or acquiescence. Or the cause of action must arise there in whole or in part (para 2).

Section 104 of the Patents Act only fixes the level of court. Section 22 of the Designs Act, 2000 works the same way. A suit cannot be filed in a court below a District Court. It goes to the High Court if a counter claim for revocation is made.

Sotkon tried to bridge the gap by adding a copyright claim over its brochure drawings. The court rejected this. It held that "jurisdiction needs to be established for each relief prayed for" (para 10.5).

Sotkon: one defendant in Delhi was not enough

Sotkon, a Spanish company, held an Indian patent for an underground refuse collection system. It alleged that a joint venture's bid in a Gandhinagar Municipal Corporation tender infringed the patent. The joint venture was in Ahmedabad. Its partners were in Mumbai and Delhi. The municipal corporation was in Gandhinagar (paras 10.1 to 10.3).

Sotkon relied on Section 20(b) CPC because one defendant had its registered office in Delhi. That clause requires either leave of the court or the acquiescence of the other defendants. No leave had been obtained. The defendants had objected to jurisdiction in their written statement, so they had not acquiesced (para 10.6).

The court then asked whether leave should be granted. It held that forum conveniens should guide that decision (para 10.7). The Madras, Bombay and Calcutta High Courts take this approach under Clause 12 of their Letters Patent. The dispute concerned a tender for bins in Gujarat. The court said: "Nothing has actually happened within the jurisdiction of this Court" (para 10.10). It refused leave and declined to entertain the suit.

Forum conveniens now applies to IP suits

Four patent suits failed on forum conveniens even where some link with Delhi was pleaded. The court asked where the "material, integral and essential part of the cause of action" arose (para 7.11).

In the HLE Glascoat and Mold-Tek suits, the defendants made the products in Gujarat. The substance of the dispute lay there (paras 8.3 and 9.7). In the Novartis dabrafenib suit, the court accepted that a part of the cause of action arose in Delhi. A part of "similar strength and character" also existed in Mumbai, where the Indian plaintiff held the import licence (paras 11.3 and 11.4).

On sample orders the court was direct. What happened in Delhi "could very well have occurred at Arunachal Pradesh, Ladakh or Kanyakumari" (para 7.11). An investigator's order delivered to Delhi did not make Delhi the right forum.

The court traced the doctrine to Lord Denning's words in Smith Kline & French Laboratories v Bloch. The plaintiff "has no longer an inborn right to choose his own forum" (para 17).

Quia timet patent suits need a place too

Plusplus Lifescience brought a quia timet suit on a nutraceutical patent and trade mark. The goods were made in Dehradun. The plaint said doctors in Delhi had been given samples, but never said where the samples were obtained (paras 15.7 and 15.8).

The court called it "one of the prime examples of brilliantly clever drafting" (para 15.9). A cause of action for a quia timet suit existed. It had not been shown to arise in Delhi.

The trade mark angle: DAPLO and DAPLOGIN

Dr Reddy's sued over DAPLOGIN, said to be similar to its DAPLO mark for dapagliflozin. It relied on its Delhi branch office under Section 134(2). Because it also claimed passing off, which Section 134(2) does not cover, that provision could not carry the whole suit (para 14.3).

Its trap purchase also failed. The invoice was issued by an online pharmacy in Bhopal to a buyer in Delhi. It was a single sale and was not made by the defendant or on its website. The court held that Banyan Tree "requires a transaction with the defendant" (para 14.6).

How India compares with the US, UK and the UPC

ForumWhere a patent suit can be brought
IndiaSection 20 CPC: where a defendant resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arises. Forum conveniens now filters the choice in Delhi.
United States28 U.S.C. § 1400(b): where the defendant resides, or where it infringed and has a regular and established place of business. TC Heartland (2017) held a domestic company resides only in its state of incorporation.
United KingdomEngland and Wales: the Patents Court or IPEC. IPEC caps damages at £500,000 and costs at £60,000. Scotland and Northern Ireland have separate courts. Territorial venue rarely arises.
Unified Patent CourtArticle 33 UPCA: the local or regional division where infringement occurred, or where a defendant resides or has its principal place of business.

The US narrowed patent venue by statute in 2017, through TC Heartland. Delhi now reaches a similar result through judicial discretion. Both look for a real link between the forum and the dispute.

What patent owners should do now

  • Plead each relief separately. Show where the cause of action arises for the patent claim, not only for a copyright or trade mark claim.
  • Seek leave under Section 20(b) at filing. If one defendant is in the chosen forum and others are not, apply for leave and explain why that forum is suitable.
  • Start from the place of manufacture. The court treated the place of making as the substance of a patent dispute.
  • Do not rely on sample orders. A delivery that could be made to any city does not show where the dispute lies.
  • File evidence for every averment. Claims that goods are sold through distributors in Delhi need documents (para 14.4).

What the judgment does not decide

The court did not rule on infringement or validity in any of the ten suits. The plaints were returned, not dismissed. Each plaintiff may present its plaint before the appropriate court. As we noted in our ABD Maestro article, a related jurisdiction question has been referred to a larger Bench in another matter. An appeal from this judgment is also possible.

Frequently asked questions

Where can a patent infringement suit be filed in India?

Under Section 20 CPC, where a defendant resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arises in whole or in part. It must be filed in a District Court or above, under Section 104 of the Patents Act.

Can a patent owner sue where it has its own office?

Not on that ground alone. The Patents Act has no provision like Section 134(2) of the Trade Marks Act. Adding a copyright claim does not change that for the patent relief.

Does a sample purchase in Delhi give the Delhi High Court jurisdiction?

Ordinarily no. In Sotkon and the connected suits, sample orders and investigator purchases did not make Delhi the proper forum. The court looked for where the substance of the dispute lay.
